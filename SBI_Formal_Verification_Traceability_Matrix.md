## 1. Purpose

This matrix provides bidirectional traceability between the conceptual statements in the SBI derivation and their manuscript-facing Lean interfaces.

Its purpose is not merely to show that similarly named objects exist in the manuscript and in Lean. Each row asks four separate questions:

1. **Identification:** Is the conceptual statement uniquely identified and sourced?
2. **Formal representation:** Is there a specific Lean declaration whose statement or type represents that conceptual claim?
3. **Semantic fidelity:** Does the Lean declaration express the same claim, or is it stronger, weaker, indirect, or dependent on a semantic encoding choice?
4. **Formal verification:** Does the claimed result follow in Lean from the stated formal inputs, with the project building successfully?

This separation is essential. Lean verifies deductive correctness of the encoded formal statements. It does not establish the physical truth of A1–A3, and it does not by itself establish that the chosen formal encoding is the unique or physically correct interpretation of the conceptual statement.

## 2. NASA-style tailoring

The matrix is patterned on the Requirements Verification Matrix approach in the NASA Systems Engineering Handbook and on NASA software-assurance traceability practice. NASA verification matrices identify requirements by unique identifiers, retain their definitive sources, state success criteria, identify the verification method, and record verification results/evidence. NASA software practice also emphasizes bidirectional traceability from higher-level requirements through software requirements/design/code.

This is a scientific formalization rather than a flight project, so several NASA hardware-oriented fields are not applicable. Fields such as test facility, preflight acceptance, and performing organization are replaced by fields that serve the same audit function for formal mathematics: Lean interface, source file, conceptual inputs, semantic-fidelity criterion, verification method, and evidence/result.

### Verification-method codes

The standard NASA verification-method vocabulary is retained and tailored as follows:

- **I — Inspection:** direct comparison of the conceptual statement with the Lean declaration and its comments/type.
- **A — Analysis:** examination of the Lean proof, dependency structure, or logical reduction to supporting declarations.
- **T — Test:** successful clean `lake build` / elaboration of the checked Lean project.
- **D — Demonstration:** normally not applicable to a static formal proof; reserved for executable demonstrations if introduced later.

Most theorem rows therefore use **I+A+T**. Pure definitions or semantic contracts normally use **I+T**. Explanatory scope statements may require **I** or **I+A+T**, depending on whether the scope statement has explicit formal counterparts.

### Result/status codes

- **PASS** — conceptual statement and manuscript-facing Lean interface agree at the level relevant to the derivation.
- **PASS+** — Lean establishes the manuscript claim and additionally proves a stronger or more explicit result; the strengthening is documented.
- **UPDATE** — the Lean formalization is internally coherent, but the current manuscript wording or dependency statement requires alignment.
- **ENCODING** — the row is a semantic/representational contract rather than a physical axiom or derived theorem; its adequacy is established by conceptual inspection, not by Lean alone.
- **EXPLANATORY** — scope/non-derivability statement for which no dedicated Lean theorem is required.

## 4. Bidirectional traceability audit

### Conceptual statement → Lean

Every numbered Definition, Lemma, Proposition, Theorem, and Remark in the current S2 proof spine now has a manuscript-facing row in the matrix. Conceptual semantic bridges that are used in proofs but are not physical axioms are represented by explicit `SEM` rows. This prevents semantic encoding choices from being hidden inside theorem hypotheses.

### Lean → conceptual statement

The matrix intentionally lists manuscript-facing interfaces rather than every helper declaration. Internal helper definitions and technical lemmas remain available in the declaration-level dependency graph. A Lean declaration belongs in this matrix when it either:

1. directly represents a conceptual manuscript statement;
2. constitutes an explicit semantic bridge needed to interpret a manuscript statement; or
3. is a public theorem that certifies one of the manuscript’s principal conclusions.

Implementation-only helpers remain in the raw dependency report rather than being promoted into the conceptual architecture.
